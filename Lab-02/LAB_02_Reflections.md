# Reflection Questions

## 1. Did your estimations reflect the actual effort?

Yes, the story-point estimations were reasonably aligned with the expected effort of the work items. Smaller tasks such as preventing duplicate feature flag keys and caching evaluation results were estimated lower, while more complex tasks such as evaluating feature flags for users and synchronizing flag changes with client SDKs were given higher estimates. Since this was a simulated sprint, the estimates mainly represented the relative complexity and effort of each task rather than actual development time.

## 2. Was your backlog well-prioritized?

Yes, the backlog was prioritized based on dependencies and functionality. The initial sprint focused on foundational features such as creating feature flags, authentication, environment configuration, and targeting rules. The second sprint then focused on feature flag evaluation, failure handling, caching, kill-switch functionality, and real-time synchronization. This ordering allowed the basic functionality to be established before moving to more advanced features.

## 3. How did your simulated sprint align with your plan?

The simulated sprints followed the planned structure. Sprint 1 focused mainly on feature flag creation, configuration, authentication, and targeting. Sprint 2 built on this foundation by implementing evaluation, error handling, caching, kill-switch overrides, environment-specific states, and SDK synchronization. All planned work items were completed, so the simulated execution aligned well with the planned backlog and sprint organization.

## 4. What insights did the burndown chart give about your team’s capacity?

The burndown chart showed that the team was able to complete all the planned story points within the simulated sprint. The remaining work dropped from the initial 21 story points to 0 for Sprint 1, indicating that the planned workload was achievable. Sprint 2 contained 26 story points and was also completed successfully. The chart helped demonstrate the relationship between the amount of planned work and the team's simulated capacity. However, because this was a simulation and the tasks were completed very quickly, the chart does not represent a real-world development velocity or actual time-based productivity.
